# dKV-Quant: Step-Aware Low-Bit KV-Cache Quantization for Diffusion LLMs

> A research proposal generated by a multi-agent literature + feasibility study,
> aligned with Prof. **Sai Qian Zhang**'s lab (NYU Tandon ECE / Courant CS —
> efficient deep learning & algorithm–hardware co-design) and the hottest 2026
> efficiency trends.

---

## 1. One-paragraph pitch

Diffusion LLMs (dLLMs) such as **LLaDA-8B** and **Dream-7B** are the fastest-rising
alternative to autoregressive (AR) LLMs, and a large 2026 literature has sprung
up to make their *KV cache* cheaper. But every one of those methods
(Fast-dLLM, dLLM-Cache, DPad, d²Cache, EntropyCache, MaskKV) attacks only **how
often / how many** entries to recompute — they keep the retained keys/values in
**full FP16**. In parallel, a mature AR literature compresses KV caches to 2–4
bits (KIVI, RotateKV, MixKVQ, XQuant…), but every one of those methods assumes a
**write-once, causal cache**: a key is computed once and never changes. dLLMs
break that assumption — bidirectional attention re-encodes the same token
positions at every denoising step, so per-channel ranges and outlier channels
**drift step to step**, and a scale calibrated at step 0 is stale by step *N*.
**dKV-Quant** is the first training-free, GPU-software KV-cache *quantizer*
purpose-built for dLLMs: it turns the warm-step recomputation the model already
performs into a free, data-less calibration probe and tracks the drift with a
lightweight EMA, recovering most of the accuracy a frozen AR quantizer loses —
and it **stacks multiplicatively** with the existing reuse methods (27× reuse ×
4× bit-compression).

## 3. Why this is a 2026 trend (not a stale topic)

The dLLM-efficiency field is exploding but has split into two tracks that have
**not been joined**:

- **Track A — dLLM cache reuse/eviction** (crowded, hot): Fast-dLLM
  (2505.22618, up to 27.6×), dLLM-Cache (2506.06295), DPad (2508.14148, 61.4×),
  d²Cache (ICLR '26, 2509.23094), EntropyCache (2603.18489), MaskKV
  (2510.09309). *All keep KV in FP16.*
- **Track B — KV-cache quantization** (mature, but AR-only): KIVI, RotateKV
  (2501.16383), MixKVQ (2512.19206), XQuant (2510.11236), KVLinC (2510.05373),
  VecInfer, TurboQuant (ICLR '26).
- **dLLM weight/activation PTQ** is just emerging — "Quantization Meets dLLMs"
  (2508.14896) finds dLLM-specific activation outliers; **Quant-dLLM** (ICLR '26,
  2510.03274) does 2-bit *weight* PTQ — but **explicitly leaves the KV cache
  untouched**.
- The **only** dLLM-specific KV-quant signal so far is **BAOS**, buried inside a
  7nm NPU hardware paper (2601.20706), in MX hardware formats — no portable,
  GPU-software algorithm exists.

**The white space is exact:** training-free, GPU-side, low-bit KV-cache
*quantization* designed for the denoising-step dynamics of dLLMs, and its
composition with the Track-A reuse machinery.

## 4. Hypotheses

- **H1 (drift).** In a bidirectional dLLM, per-channel KV statistics and the set
  of outlier channels drift materially across denoising steps — unlike an AR
  causal cache. *(Measurable with no GPU; our mock-model profiler already shows
  ~15% step-to-step tensor drift and ~13% outlier-channel churn — see §7.)*
- **H2 (AR quantizers break).** Dropping a write-once AR KV-quantizer onto a dLLM
  loses significant accuracy at 3–4 bits because its step-0 calibration goes
  stale.
- **H3 (step-aware recovers it).** Recalibrating from the model's own warm-step
  recomputation (EMA over steps) recovers most of the gap to a fresh-every-step
  oracle, at negligible extra cost. *(Our CPU simulation: rel-L2 0.078 for
  step-aware vs 0.137 for AR-static vs 0.069 oracle at 4-bit — see §7.)*
- **H4 (orthogonality).** Quantized storage composes with Track-A reuse: applying
  dKV-Quant on top of Fast-dLLM/d²Cache yields ~bit-compression× additional
  memory savings at matched accuracy.

## 5. Method (what we build)

1. **Step-aware calibration.** Maintain per-channel (or per-group) quantization
   scales as an EMA across denoising steps. Each step's warm recomputation
   supplies a fresh min/max probe — no calibration data needed. `momentum`
   interpolates between fresh-every-step (m=0) and frozen-AR (m=1).
2. **Outlier handling.** Optional fast **Hadamard rotation** of the head-dim
   before quantization (RotateKV/KVLinC/DFRot lineage) to spread channel
   outliers — exact and invertible, so undone on dequant.
3. **Mixed-precision allocation (stretch).** Use dLLM-native importance signals
   (decoded-token entropy from EntropyCache; mask-query attention from MaskKV) to
   assign per-token/per-channel bit-widths — the dLLM analogue of MixKVQ.
4. **Reuse × quantize integration.** Plug the quantized store into Fast-dLLM's
   block-wise approximate cache so reuse and low-bit storage stack.

All of the above is prototyped in **fp32 simulated (fake) quantization** first
(CPU, no GPU), then validated on real LLaDA/Dream activations, then (optional
systems paper) realized with true low-bit kernels.

## 6. Experimental plan

**Models:** `GSAI-ML/LLaDA-8B-Instruct` (MIT), `Dream-org/Dream-v0-Instruct-7B`
(Apache-2.0), optionally `inclusionAI/LLaDA-MoE-7B-A1B`.
**Baselines:** FP16 cache; naive AR KV-quant (KIVI/RotateKV-style, our
`ar_static`); BAOS-style; Track-A reuse alone (Fast-dLLM).
**Benchmarks:** GSM8K, MATH, HumanEval, MBPP, MMLU, LongBench/BBH.
**Metrics:** task accuracy; throughput (tok/s); tokens-per-NFE; peak KV memory
(bits) & compression ratio; **AUP** (accuracy-under-parallelism, 2601.07568).

**Main tables.** (T1) accuracy vs bit-width {2,3,4,8} × method; (T2) reuse-alone
vs reuse×quant memory/accuracy; (T3) ablation momentum & Hadamard; (T4)
per-model & per-task generalization. **Main figure:** the §7 drift curves.

## 7. What is already done in this repo (no GPU)

The numpy core is implemented, tested (19 passing), and **runs**:

- `dkv_quant/quantizers.py` — uniform fake-quant (per-tensor/channel/token/group,
  sym/asym), Hadamard rotation, and the three calibration policies
  (`KVQuantizer` fresh, `StaticARQuantizer` frozen-AR, `StepAwareKVQuantizer` ours).
- `dkv_quant/mock_dllm.py` — a tiny **bidirectional** masked-diffusion transformer
  (random weights) reproducing the structural facts that cause KV drift.
- `dkv_quant/denoise.py` — LLaDA-style block / semi-AR denoising loop with
  low-confidence remasking, integrated with the quantizing cache.
- `dkv_quant/profiler.py` — measures step-to-step KV drift, scale drift, and
  outlier churn (the H1 evidence engine).
- `dkv_quant/kv_cache.py`, `metrics.py`, `baselines.py`, `data.py` — quantized
  cache store + memory accounting, all metrics (incl. AUP), method registry,
  benchmark/calibration loaders.

**Reproduced on CPU right now:**
```
KV-cache drift (mock dLLM):  k_tensor_drift≈0.15  outlier_churn≈0.13   → supports H1
Sim quant @4-bit (rel-L2):   ar_static 0.137 │ dkv_step_aware 0.078 │ fresh 0.069
                                              └─ ours nearly halves AR error → supports H2/H3
```

## 8. GPU vs no-GPU split

**No GPU (done / runnable here):** literature & gap analysis; fake-quant
simulation; cache-drift profiling; metrics/AUP; method registry; eval-harness &
data plumbing; the entire experimental scaffold.
**GPU (the only remaining work):** real LLaDA/Dream inference; running Track-A
baselines; calibration-based PTQ; final accuracy benchmarks; (optional) true
low-bit kernels. No large-scale **training** anywhere — every technique is
training-free PTQ / training-free decoding.

## 9. Risks & mitigations

- *Drift might be small on real models.* → The profiler runs on real LLaDA the
  moment a GPU is available; if drift is layer-localized, switch to a
  per-layer/per-head momentum (the code already keeps per-layer quantizers).
- *AR methods might already be "good enough" at 4-bit.* → Push to 3/2-bit and to
  long-context, where stale calibration hurts most (mirrors MixKVQ's setting).
- *Reuse × quant might interact non-trivially.* → That interaction *is* a result;
  measure it (T2) rather than assume independence.
